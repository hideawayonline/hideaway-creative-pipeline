# Hook principles

A hook holds attention when it works with how a scrolling viewer's mind behaves, not because of its wording. Use these principles as a lens. To judge or build a hook, ask which ones it satisfies and which it breaks. Each states the mechanism, so you can apply it to cases not listed here.

## Getting the viewer to stop

1. **Autopilot interruption.** Scrolling runs on habit. Something has to register as new or out of pattern before the swipe happens. A small visual or situational shift is enough. Size matters less than difference from the feed.
2. **Fast keep-or-leave decision.** The viewer makes a quick conscious call in the first few seconds. They are testing for relevance and interest, not patiently waiting for a story to start. Early moments get judged as a pitch.
3. **Emotion before information.** Feeling comes before teaching. A viewer who feels nothing has no hold on them, so the first beat should trigger a reaction such as surprise, amusement, worry, or desire. Explanation comes after.
4. **Perceptual salience, with restraint.** Motion, contrast, odd objects, and clear focal points catch the eye. Too many competing elements overload the viewer, who then doesn't know where to look. One clear lead element beats several.

## Giving them a reason to stay

5. **Two silent questions.** The viewer is asking "what is this about?" and "what do I get if I stay?" A hook holds when it answers both quickly: a clear subject and a visible payoff.
6. **Self-relevance and stakes.** People stay when the outcome matters to them, whether as a wanted result, an avoided pain, or a person they care about. Speaking to a broad shared concern keeps more viewers in, because anyone who decides "not for me" is gone. Narrowing can happen later.
7. **Curiosity gap.** An unanswered question that only continuing can resolve is the core engine of attention. The mind wants to close open loops. A new loop opened as one closes keeps the pull going.
8. **Violated expectation and contrast.** The viewer holds a belief or baseline, and a surprising alternative opens a gap between the two. The bigger the gap, the stronger the pull to reconcile it. This also stops autopilot.
9. **Withhold the resolution.** If the opening lets the viewer guess the answer or hands over the payoff, the tension is released and they feel they already got what they came for. The answer must come later than the question.
10. **Tension and anticipation.** Obstacles, escalating stakes, and a visible structure that promises a payoff (a countdown, a list, a stated goal) give the viewer something specific to wait for. Skipping ahead would spoil it.

## Keeping them from leaving

11. **Curious, not confused.** Curiosity needs understanding: the viewer knows what is happening and only wonders what comes next. Confusion feels like work and prompts a swipe. Give enough context to orient them and keep one thing unknown.
12. **Low cognitive load.** Working memory is small. Short, plain, single-meaning language is absorbed instantly. Even half a second of effort makes leaving the cheaper option. Compress, but never so fast that the idea can't register.
13. **Aligned channels, and meaning without sound.** Visuals, on-screen text, speech, and sound should say the same thing, so comprehension is instant and nothing contradicts. Many viewers watch muted, and the eyes take in meaning faster than the ears, so the picture and text must carry the idea alone.
14. **Show, don't tell.** A concrete, visible scenario or result is felt and understood at once. Specific details feel real and worth acting on, while vague generalities feel like filler.
15. **Immersion with no dead space.** Starting inside the action removes a warm-up the viewer could bail on. Pauses, preamble, and filler between beats give natural exit points.

## Earning trust

16. **Credibility.** Viewers have seen many similar videos and ask why they should believe this one. Proof, visible results, and authority signals make the promise feel real. An unsupported claim reads as bait.
17. **Promise-delivery integrity.** Right after the hook, the viewer checks whether the content matches what was teased. A match relaxes them and keeps them. A bait-and-switch or a delayed payoff feels like being stalled or tricked, and they leave. Rising value after the hook helps: each beat should seem at least as good as the last.
18. **Familiarity and authenticity.** A known format with a twist brings comfort plus novelty. Viewers also detect fakeness fast, and once trust breaks it taints everything after.
