3. Withdrawing my request for two or three more days

The experiment has already run and I did not read it properly. Deep discounting started on 24 August. Two full weeks later, orders are flat at 459 then 509, against 1,245 in the last normal week. The discounting has not bought volume. It has only given away margin. Waiting another three days tells us nothing we do not already know, and costs us roughly another A$1,800 a day.

5. The part neither of us has been solving

Cutting spend cannot make paid acquisition profitable on its own. Here is the whole life of a customer we buy today.

 The A$19.70 is the 24.8% of customers who ever buy again, times the 1.73 further orders they average, times A$46. Meta only, before Google, TikTok, affiliates and creator fees.

We lose money on a customer across their entire life with us, not just on the first order. Only one in four ever comes back, and that has not moved in two years. Fix the return rate to the industry floor of 30% and the same customer turns profitable without any change to what we pay for them. That is the same lever that fixes the conversion rate, because returning customers are the visitors who convert at 22% and above.

What I need from you

6. The decision

  1. Cut the five campaigns below break-even today. About A$7,000 a week of cash back, immediately.
  2. Stop the clearance discounting. Two weeks proves it is not buying volume.
  3. Keep clearance and retargeting running. They are the only spend above water.
  4. Agree the second purchase rate as a reported metric alongside the five. It is the one number that fixes both the marketing efficiency ratio and the conversion rate structurally.

If you want the two break-even campaigns cut as well, say so and I will do it. That is another A$5,000 a week of cash and costs us almost no profit.

One thing to confirm before we treat this as the new normal

Our Meta account was effectively dark from 17 to 23 August, spending A$173 across the whole week. Restarting after a blackout resets the learning phases and depresses efficiency for a while. Some of the drop since then may be that rather than a structural collapse. I am finding out what happened that week before drawing a longer conclusion from the last fortnight.

Method and limits

  • Sales, orders, units and discounts: Shopify analytics for hideaway.online, weekly, queried 9 September 2026. Net sales is after discounts and refunds, before GST and shipping.
  • Ad spend and returns: HW 2 Meta account, weekly totals and campaign results for 24 August to 8 September. Meta counts conversions on its own attribution window and generally claims more than Shopify records, so treat the ratios as directional and the spend as exact.
  • Conversion: Shopify sessions from 27 August 2026, when session tracking began, to 8 September. Only thirteen days, and Shopify recognises a visitor only when they are logged in or arrive from an email, which flatters the known-customer figure. The size of the gap is not in doubt; the exact multiple is.
  • Per order costs: house averages of A$20.50 goods and A$14 shipping, from the affiliate audit model, not Xero actuals. If the true contribution is A$40 rather than A$46, every figure here worsens and none of the conclusions change.
  • Return rate and lifetime value: from the customer cohort analysis in the return rate report, covering customers first acquired between September 2024 and August 2025.
